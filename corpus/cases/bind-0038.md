# Russia 149-FZ decoding-key duty vs. E2EE messaging

Organizers that encrypt users' messages, or let users encrypt them, must give the FSB the information needed to decode them. The same article requires messengers to keep messages confidential.

## Instruments
- mandating: 149-FZ Art. 10.1(4.1), inserted by Federal Law No. 374-FZ of 06.07.2016 (https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_61798/9ab7abe2b9fe407f507610f7e6e14a951d575585/)
- conflicting: 149-FZ Art. 10.1(4.2)(4), inserted by Federal Law No. 241-FZ of 29.07.2017 (https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_61798/9ab7abe2b9fe407f507610f7e6e14a951d575585/)

## Analysis
Art. 10.1(4.1) applies when the service offers users the ability to encode messages. In E2EE the session keys are generated and held on endpoints. The organizer has no decoding information to hand over unless it redesigns to capture keys, which is key escrow. Art. 10.1(4.2)(4) in the same article requires instant-messaging organizers to ensure message confidentiality. A service can meet 4.1 only by holding keys, and holding keys makes every message readable by the organizer and anyone who compromises it.

## Inclusion
Endpoint-held keys and a duty to supply decoding information can't both hold. Any mechanism that lets the organizer supply keys makes the organizer able to decrypt. Distinct from N12: a standing statutory duty with no notice or review step.
