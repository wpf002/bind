# Differential privacy budget vs. redistricting accuracy

Census Bureau reconstruction tests show block-level tables accurate enough for equal-population districting let an attacker rebuild individual records, which 13 U.S.C. § 9(a)(2) forbids publishing. The privacy budget sets where that trade lands, and no setting satisfies both. Neither statute sets an accuracy floor or a privacy-loss limit.

## Instruments
- mandating: Title 13 13 U.S.C. § 9(a)(2) (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/13/9)
- mandating: PL 94-171 13 U.S.C. § 141(c) (Pub. L. 94-171) (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/13/141)
- context: Alabama v. Commerce Alabama v. U.S. Dep't of Commerce, No. 3:21-cv-211 (M.D. Ala. June 29, 2021) (three-judge court), Doc. 139 (https://redistricting.lls.edu/wp-content/uploads/AL-commerce2-20210629-motion-deny-pi.pdf)

## Analysis
2020 US Census: Title 13 confidentiality vs. PL 94-171 redistricting accuracy. Litigated (Alabama v. Commerce). The court denied relief on standing and ripeness without reaching the merits.

The accuracy demand comes from equal-population case law, not PL 94-171: Karcher v. Daggett allows no avoidable deviation in congressional districts. Karcher also measures equality against the census count as published, which a reviewer needs to weigh against this statement. Whether swapping or other methods would meet § 9 is the Bureau's judgment and is disputed.
