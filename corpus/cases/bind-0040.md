# DSA Art. 28 age assurance robustness vs. no additional data

The Art. 28 guidelines reject age checks minors can easily get around. Art. 28(3) says platforms aren't obliged to process additional personal data to tell whether a user is a minor.

## Instruments
- mandating: DSA Art. 28(3) (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065)
- context: Art. 28 Guidelines Para 46 (https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/118226)
- conflicting: GDPR Art. 5(1)(c) (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679)

## Analysis
Distinct from the age-verification vs. Art. 5(1)(c) case: this one is internal to Art. 28 and its guidelines. Para 41 and para 46 resolve the platform side with double-blind tokens. The platform learns only that the threshold is met, and the issuer doesn't learn which service. The remaining question is § 6.1.4(c) robustness. A bearer token says a valid credential was presented. It doesn't say who presented it, so an adult's token or wallet can be used by a minor. Binding the proof to the person at presentation takes a liveness or biometric check, which is the extra processing 28(3) and § 6.1.4(d) push against. Device-local binding keeps that data off the platform. Whether it counts as robust enough is the Commission's call, and the guidelines leave it case by case.

## Inclusion
Likely fails as resolved_by_known_technique if a reviewer accepts device-local holder binding as robust. Kept undecided because the guidelines set robustness case by case and no regulator has ruled on token sharing.
