# DMA 6(7) Wi-Fi interoperability vs. single-account trust domain

Apple must let third-party connected devices auto-join the user's Wi-Fi networks. Today it shares saved Wi-Fi passwords only with devices on the same Apple Account.

## Instruments
- mandating: DMA Art. 6(7), second subparagraph (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R1925)
- mandating: DMA.100203 C(2025) 3000 final, 19.3.2025, para 107 (https://ec.europa.eu/competition/digital_markets_act/cases/202523/DMA_100203_1655.pdf)
- context: GDPR Art. 5(1)(f) (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679)

## Analysis
The specification decision lists the shared data: SSID, BSSID and the network password. Apple's trust boundary for that secret is the Apple Account. A third-party watch has its own vendor account and its own vendor cloud. Para 107 says integrity under the DMA "does not allow gatekeepers to impose their own model of security and privacy". So Apple can't require the device to join its account, and must release a credential to a device whose trust root it doesn't control. Every new vendor is a new root that holds the user's network password.

## Inclusion
Releasing the password to a device outside the account is the mandate. The single-account boundary is the property. Per-device consent prompts don't restore it, since the secret still leaves the domain. Only a level-down (shrinking Apple's own feature) changes the equation, and that drops the property for first-party devices.
