# Lawful-access key escrow against forward secrecy

Intercept-capability duties presume a key that decrypts recorded sessions; forward secrecy's guarantee is that no such long-lived key exists.

## Instruments
- mandating: CALEA § 1002(a) (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/1002)
- context: 3GPP TS 33.501 § 6.12 (https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/Specs/archive/33_series/33.501/)

## Analysis
TLS 1.3 and the Signal ratchet derive per-session keys and discard them, so recorded ciphertext stays unreadable after the fact even to the operator. An escrowed key reintroduces exactly the single high-value target the property removes, and the escrow holder becomes a standing compromise path. This is the structural reason retrospective access and forward secrecy cannot coexist.

## Inclusion
Retrospective decryption requires key retention. Key retention is the negation of the property. No budget reconciles a property defined as the absence of the thing being required.
