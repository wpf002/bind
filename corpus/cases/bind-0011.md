# Right to repair vs. parts pairing / attested firmware

Secure boot and component attestation work by rejecting unattested parts. Repair requires installing exactly those.

## Instruments
- mandating: EU R2R Art. 5(6) (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024L1799)
- mandating: US States Or. SB 1596 (2024), Or. Laws 2024 ch. 69, § 1(2)(b) (https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2024R1/Downloads/MeasureDocument/SB1596/Enrolled)

## Analysis
Review: Directive 2024/1799 Art. 5(6) exempts techniques 'justified by legitimate and objective factors'. If security attestation qualifies, the directive resolves the conflict. Verdict decided as passes on 2026-11-05 because the US laws carry no such exemption. Review by claude-opus-5-5, 2026-10-31, pending human countersign.

Amended from v0.1 by claude-opus-5-5, 2026-10-31. Signed off by Will Foti, 2026-11-01, on claude-opus-5-5's source verification.
inclusionReasoning: "No, not without a plural attestation model that doesn't currently exist." Verdict decided by claude-opus-5-5 and approved by the owner, 2026-11-05.

## Inclusion
No. Attestation works by rejecting parts the manufacturer didn't attest, and a repair mandate requires accepting them. Budget doesn't change that. A plural attestation model would replace the architecture, not satisfy it. Directive 2024/1799 Art. 5(6) may exempt security-justified pairing in the EU; the US state laws have no such exemption.
