# DMA 6(8) independent ad verification vs. data minimization

Gatekeepers must give advertisers non-aggregated data for their own independent verification of ad inventory.

## Instruments
- mandating: DMA Art. 6(8) (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R1925)
- conflicting: DMA Art. 8(1) (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R1925)
- conflicting: GDPR Art. 5(1)(c) (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679)

## Analysis
Per-impression data about end users goes to advertisers and their verification vendors. The minimization side is weaker than it looks. Art. 6(8) is a legal obligation, which gives a GDPR Art. 6(1)(c) basis, and minimization is measured against the verification purpose, which the statute says needs non-aggregated data. Recital 47 also recognises fraud prevention as a legitimate interest. The residual problem: "independent" verification run over logs the gatekeeper produced can only check the logs against themselves. Real independence needs a second observation point per impression, which is a new collection the DMA doesn't authorise. Whether the statute requires that level of independence is unclear.

## Inclusion
If Art. 6(8) only requires the gatekeeper to hand over its own logs, GDPR Art. 6(1)(c) and a purpose-limited clean environment satisfy both sides and the case fails. If independent verification means an observation the gatekeeper doesn't control, it needs a second per-impression collection and may pass. Needs a legal read of 'independent'.
