# CERT-In VPN subscriber records vs. no-log VPN

India's 2022 CERT-In Directions make consumer VPN providers register validated subscriber identities and the IPs each member used, and keep them five years. A no-log VPN keeps no record linking a user to an IP or session.

## Instruments
- mandating: CERT-In Directions Para (v), items a-g; para (iv) (https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf)
- context: CERT-In FAQ Q34; Q35 (https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/FAQs_on_CyberSecurityDirections_May2022.pdf)

## Analysis
Para (v) requires validated names, addresses, hire periods and "IPs allotted to / being used by the members", held for 5 years after the account closes. Para (iv) adds 180 days of ICT system logs. FAQ Q34 limits (v) to providers of Internet proxy-like services to the general public, which is the consumer VPN market. A no-log, anonymous-signup VPN is built so the operator can't answer who used an exit IP at a given time. Records of validated identity plus allotted IPs are the answer to that question. A provider can't hold the records and also lack them. The FAQ's offshore-storage allowance in Q35 changes where the records sit. They still exist.

## Inclusion
The technical conflict is total: shared-exit, no-log designs can't produce member-to-IP records. Undecided because the corpus hasn't settled whether a conflict between one mandate and a product's security property, with no opposing law, meets the inclusion test. Several providers reportedly removed Indian servers in 2022; press-reported, not verified.
