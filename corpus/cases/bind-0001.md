# Erasure of personal data recorded on a public distributed ledger

GDPR Art. 17 requires a controller to erase personal data on request; a public ledger's security property is that no participant can remove a committed entry.

## Instruments
- mandating: GDPR Art. 17(1) (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- context: Certificate Transparency § 2 (https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html)

## Analysis
The ledger's value to every relying party is that history cannot be rewritten by anyone, including the party who wrote it. Erasure capability is therefore not a missing feature but the inverse of the guarantee. Pruning by consensus would require coordinated action by parties with no obligation to the data subject and no way to be compelled jointly. Off-chain storage with on-chain hashes narrows the exposure but leaves a persistent linkable commitment.

## Inclusion
Unlimited budget buys neither consensus from independent validators nor the retroactive removal of data already replicated to nodes outside any controller's reach.
