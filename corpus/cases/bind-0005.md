# Erasure from signed commit history in public repositories

Author identity is embedded in commit objects and covered by commit signatures; GDPR Art. 17 erasure requires rewriting objects whose immutability every clone and signature depends on.

## Instruments
- mandating: GDPR Art. 17(1) (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)

## Analysis
A commit hash covers the author field, so changing it produces a different commit and invalidates every descendant hash and signature. Rewriting published history breaks all existing clones, tags, and release attestations. Forks and mirrors outside the controller's reach retain the original objects regardless.

## Inclusion
History rewriting is technically available and still fails: the controller cannot reach independent clones, and the rewrite destroys the integrity property that made the record evidence.
