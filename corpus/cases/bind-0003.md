# Erasure of names embedded in Certificate Transparency log entries

Certificates carry subject names that can be personal data, and CT logs are append-only by design; erasure under GDPR Art. 17 would break the property browsers rely on.

## Instruments
- mandating: GDPR Art. 17(1) (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- conflicting: Certificate Transparency § 2 (https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html)

## Analysis
A CT log's signed tree head is a promise that no entry was removed. Removing one invalidates every inclusion proof issued against the affected tree and is detectable as misbehaviour, which is the mechanism's entire point. The data subject is frequently the certificate requester, so the exposure is self-inflicted, but the erasure right does not turn on that.

## Inclusion
Log operators cannot remove entries without being treated as faulty by clients. Shutting down the log does not remove entries already mirrored.
