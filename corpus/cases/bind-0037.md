# DSA 26(3) special-category ad ban vs. behavioural profiling

Platforms may not target ads by profiling that uses special-category data. The CJEU treats data that indirectly reveals a special category as special-category data.

## Instruments
- mandating: DSA Art. 26(3) (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065)
- context: C-252/21 Judgment of 4 July 2023, operative part, point 2 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62021CJ0252)
- context: C-184/20 Judgment of 1 August 2022, operative part, point 2 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62020CJ0184)
- conflicting: GDPR Art. 9(1) (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679)

## Analysis
C-252/21 and C-184/20 extend Art. 9 to processing that allows special-category information to be revealed, directly or by inference. Behavioural features (pages visited, follows, watch time) all carry some signal about health, religion, politics or sexuality. A learned embedding mixes them. There's no threshold in the text at which inference stops counting. Proving a model doesn't use special categories means testing it against special-category labels, which needs the Art. 9 data the rule is meant to keep out.

## Inclusion
Any behavioural profile is partly a proxy for a special category. Removing all proxy signal removes the profile. Measuring residual proxy signal needs Art. 9 data, which G5's carve-out covers only for high-risk AI bias testing, not ad targeting.
