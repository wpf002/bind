# Supplementary measures for transfers exposed to Section 702

Post-Schrems II, standard contractual clauses required supplementary measures making transferred data unreadable to US surveillance; no measure achieved that for a US provider able to decrypt.

## Instruments
- mandating: GDPR Art. 44–46 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- mandating: FISA 702 § 1881a (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/50/1881a)
- context: EU–US DPF Art. 1 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2023/1795/oj)

## Analysis
The requirement was essential equivalence against a legal power held over the importer. Encryption with importer-held keys does not defeat a demand on the importer, and encryption with exporter-held keys removes the processing the transfer existed to enable. The conflict closed in July 2023 when the Commission adopted an adequacy decision, which changed the legal predicate. No engineering advance contributed, which is the single data point the longitudinal tracking exists to accumulate.

## Inclusion
Passed the test while live. Retained after resolution because the resolution mechanism is the finding.
